- **Rocks** have no clear owner.
- **The L10** has no one chairing it.
- **The Scorecard** has numbers that nobody is accountable for.

**"We all know who does what"** is a common view, but your own checkup doesn't support it:
- **Your scores:** an hour ago, all five of you rated a current, written chart (Q9) at **1/5**, and the fit between people and roles (Q10) at **2/5**.
- **Your Q3 priorities:** they include "fix the dispatch handover chaos" and "maybe hire a second ops lead?". Both are structural questions: who owns the handover between Sales, Operations and Customer Success, and does Operations need one seat or two?
- **The open question:** your team has no Integrator yet, i.e. one person who runs the day-to-day and breaks ties between functions. With five people at 34 employees, that's usually the biggest question in the room.

**Skipping this one step still wouldn't get you to the vision work.** The V/TO™ is Step 5. It depends on Rocks, the L10 and the Scorecard (Steps 2–4), and those depend on the chart. Going straight to the vision would mean skipping all four Phase 1 steps. The order is deliberate: a team that already executes every week writes a vision it actually follows, and a team that doesn't writes a wish list.

**What I'd suggest for Maya.** Step 1 starts with the Visionary/Integrator split, which is the part that defines Maya's role. A founder who gets excited about vision is usually the Visionary seat. Settling that formally, and freeing her from day-to-day integration work, is the first concrete step towards the vision work she wants to do. The full chart takes about 60–90 minutes with all five of you, and you're all in the room now.
