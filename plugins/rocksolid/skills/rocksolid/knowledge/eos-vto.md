# The V/TO (Vision/Traction Organizer)

The V/TO™ — short for the Vision/Traction Organizer™ — is the fifth tool
installed by the Entrepreneurial Operating System™ (EOS™) and the heart of
the Vision Component. It is a deliberately compressed two-page artifact that
forces the leadership team to reach full agreement on eight questions about
who the company is, where it is going, and how it will get there over the
next twelve months and ninety days. The compression is the point: the
constraint of two pages makes it impossible for the team to hide behind
length, jargon, or aspirational vagueness. Whatever survives onto the V/TO
is what the leadership team has actually committed to. Anything that does
not fit is either not important enough to be in the vision or not yet
sharp enough to be written down.

## Why the V/TO comes after the traction tools

The most counter-intuitive thing about the V/TO is that it is implemented
fifth, not first. The natural founder instinct is to start with vision —
write the long-term plan, then back-derive everything else. The book argues
explicitly against this sequence, and the reasoning is practical rather
than philosophical.

A vision session held with a leadership team that already lives inside a
disciplined execution rhythm — Accountability Chart™ in place, Rocks™
running, weekly L10™ humming, Scorecard™ keeping everyone honest — produces
grounded, debated, realistic planning. Everyone in the room already knows
what the business can actually execute, because they have been measured
against weekly reality for months. They know what their function is capable
of, what the organization's bottlenecks are, and what kind of growth is
plausible.

A vision session held with a team that has none of those disciplines tends
to produce aspirational fluff. Without measurement, planning is wishful
thinking; without execution rhythm, vision becomes a wall poster nobody
revisits. So EOS deliberately delays the V/TO until the team has earned the
right to do it well. Discipline first, then vision becomes meaningful.

For the skill, this means the V/TO sits squarely in Phase 2, never in Phase
1, and any team that wants to "start with vision" gets gently redirected to
build the foundational tools first.

## The two-page structure

The V/TO is exactly two pages, and the two pages have different functions:

### Page 1 — the Vision page

The Vision page captures who the company is and the longer-term picture of
where it is going. Structurally, it is a two-column layout:

- **Left column (stable identity, top to bottom):**
  - Core Values
  - Core Focus™ (with two sub-fields: Purpose/Cause/Passion and Niche)
  - 10-Year Target™ (single line)
  - Marketing Strategy (a multi-field block with target market, three
    uniques, proven process, and guarantee)
- **Right column (forward picture):**
  - 3-Year Picture™ (future date, revenue, profit, measurables, and a
    bullet list describing what the business looks like)

The left/right split is deliberate and load-bearing. The left column groups
the slow-changing identity elements — values, focus, long target, marketing
positioning — that the team should be touching only rarely. The right
column holds the concrete forward picture that gets refreshed annually. The
visual separation reinforces the conceptual distinction between "who we
are" and "what we'll have built three years from now."

### Page 2 — the Traction page

The Traction page captures how the company will get there over the next
twelve months and ninety days. Structurally, it is a three-column layout:

- **Left column:** the 1-Year Plan (date, revenue target, profit target,
  measurables, and three to seven annual goals)
- **Middle column:** the current quarter's Rocks (date, revenue, profit,
  measurables, and three to seven Rocks with an explicit Who column for
  ownership)
- **Right column:** the long-term Issues List (an open numbered list of
  strategic items not urgent this quarter)

The IDS™ triad (Identify / Discuss / Solve) is visually anchored next to
the Issues column on the page itself — not as an external add-on but as a
printed reminder that the long-term Issues List is processed using the same
discipline as the weekly one.

The single most important structural rule is that the V/TO is exactly two
pages, never one and never many. Templates that collapse it into a single
page lose the conceptual separation between identity and execution.
Templates that explode it across many pages lose the forcing function of
the constraint.

## Cadence — slow on the left, fast on the right

The two pages also evolve on different cadences:

- **Vision page (page 1)** changes slowly. Core Values and Core Focus may
  not change for years. The 10-Year Target rolls forward only when the
  company gets within three years of it. The 3-Year Picture is rebuilt
  once a year at the Annual Planning Session, but everything else on the
  page is touched only when something fundamental about the business
  changes.
- **Traction page (page 2)** refreshes much more frequently. The Quarterly
  Rocks column resets every ninety days at the Quarterly Planning Session.
  The 1-Year Plan resets annually. The long-term Issues List grows
  continuously between sessions and gets processed at quarterly and
  annual gatherings.

The skill's job is to enforce both cadences: gate the team away from
re-litigating Core Values every quarter, and gate them toward refreshing
Quarterly Rocks every quarter without fail.

## The eight questions in order

The V/TO formalizes eight questions that the leadership team must reach
full agreement on. The order is fixed and the questions are facts about
the format:

1. What are the company's core values?
2. What is its core focus?
3. What is its 10-year target?
4. What is its marketing strategy?
5. What is its 3-year picture?
6. What is its 1-year plan?
7. What are the quarterly Rocks?
8. What are the current issues?

The first five live on the Vision page; the last three live on the Traction
page. The progression moves from the most stable elements (values, focus)
through the longer horizon (10-year, marketing, 3-year) and down into the
nearer-term execution (1-year, quarter, issues). Each layer is meant to be
derivable from the layer above it, so a team that completes the questions
in order produces a vertically coherent answer.

The book is explicit that this is a leadership-team exercise, not a
founder draft. The whole leadership team is in the room, and the eight
answers are the product of group debate, not a single person's opinion.

## A team off-site, not a solo draft

The V/TO is built in a single one- or two-day off-site session attended by
the entire leadership team. This is non-negotiable for several reasons:

- The questions require alignment, and alignment cannot be achieved by
  email. Every leader has to be in the room while the answers are debated.
- The off-site removes the day-to-day distractions that would otherwise
  pull the team back into operational firefighting mid-session.
- The eight questions interact with each other — Marketing Strategy
  depends on Core Focus, the 1-Year Plan depends on the 3-Year Picture,
  and so on. They cannot be answered in isolation.
- The team's commitment to the resulting V/TO is roughly proportional to
  how much each leader contributed to writing it.

A V/TO produced by the founder and circulated to the team for "input" is
not a V/TO. It is a wall poster. The skill should reject solo-authored
V/TOs as structurally invalid.

## Connection to the rest of the EOS toolkit

Each section of the V/TO ties forward into another EOS tool, which is why
building the V/TO triggers a cascade of recalibration across the rest of
the system:

- **Core Values** feed the People Analyzer™ (drafted in Phase 1 People
  Component work; formally finalized on the V/TO in Phase 2)
- **Core Focus** feeds the discipline of saying no to opportunities outside
  the sweet spot
- **10-Year Target → 3-Year Picture → 1-Year Plan → Quarterly Rocks** is
  the full vision-to-traction cascade; each layer should be checkably
  derivable from the layer above
- **Marketing Strategy → Proven Process** feeds directly into the Core
  Processes work in Tool 6 — the Proven Process is itself one of the
  documented core processes
- **1-Year Plan goals** become the source of weekly goals on the Scorecard
  in Phase 2 (replacing the pragmatic Phase 1 targets)
- **Quarterly Rocks** must match the current Rock Sheet — when the V/TO is
  newly built, a Rock Alignment Check compares the existing Rocks against
  the just-written 1-Year Plan and flags any Rocks that no longer fit
- **Issues List (long-term)** is processed at Quarterly and Annual Planning
  Sessions and feeds future Rocks and 1-Year Plan goals

The V/TO is also the artifact that gets repeatedly communicated out to the
whole organization at quarterly state-of-the-company meetings, kickoff
events, and annual all-hands sessions. Vision has to be repeated many
times before most people internalize it.

## Substance criteria

A V/TO passes the substance check when:

- All eight questions are answered, not a subset
- The artifact is exactly two pages, with the Vision page using the
  two-column left/right layout and the Traction page using the
  three-column layout
- Core Values: between three and seven, each phrased concretely enough to
  decide a hire/fire question with — not generic one-word abstractions
- Core Focus: both sub-parts present and substantive — a brief
  Purpose/Cause/Passion statement and a brief Niche statement
- 10-Year Target: a single goal, with a date and a measurable number
- Marketing Strategy: a target market filterable enough to build a
  prospect list from, exactly three uniques, a named proven process, and
  a concretely stated guarantee (or a justified decision not to have one)
- 3-Year Picture: future date, revenue, profit, measurables, and at
  least several "looks like" bullets
- 1-Year Plan: three to seven SMART goals, a revenue target, a profit
  target, measurables, and evidence that a budget supports the plan
- Quarterly Rocks: present on the V/TO and each one traceable up to the
  1-Year Plan (Rock Alignment Check passes)
- Long-term Issues List: exists and has entries
- Authorship: produced as the output of a leadership team off-site, not
  drafted by a single person and circulated for review

## Common pitfalls

- **Founder-only V/TO.** Drafting it solo and then asking the team to
  approve it. The team commitment that makes the V/TO meaningful never
  forms because the team didn't do the work.
- **Skipping questions.** Answering five of eight and calling it done.
  The interactions between questions break and the cascade stops being
  derivable.
- **Collapsing to one page.** Loses the structural distinction between
  Vision (slow, identity) and Traction (fast, execution).
- **Treating the Vision page as living.** Re-litigating Core Values or
  Core Focus every quarter signals the team didn't actually commit to
  them in the first place.
- **Treating the Traction page as static.** Failing to refresh the
  Quarterly Rocks column every ninety days collapses the entire purpose
  of the page.
- **Aspirational answers with no measurement.** Vision-fluff phrasing
  ("be the best at X," "delight customers") that cannot be checked or
  acted on.
- **Building it before the foundational tools.** Phase 1 teams that try
  to start with the V/TO produce ungrounded answers because they have
  no execution discipline yet.
- **One-and-done.** Building the V/TO once and never communicating it to
  the rest of the organization. Vision has to be repeated many times
  before it propagates.

## When to apply

- **Phase 2 kickoff**, in a one- or two-day off-site with the full
  leadership team, after the Accountability Chart, Rocks, Meeting Pulse,
  and Scorecard are in place
- **Annual Planning Session**, to refresh the 3-Year Picture and rebuild
  the 1-Year Plan
- **Quarterly Planning Session**, to refresh the Rocks column and review
  the rest of the V/TO for any drift
- **Whenever the business changes fundamentally** — acquisition, major
  pivot, leadership change — touch the Vision page elements that no
  longer describe reality
