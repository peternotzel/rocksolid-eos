# Level 10 Meeting™ Agenda (fixed template)

> **Status:** empty_template — the fixed agenda used for every L10™
> **Team-required:** yes (see `rocksolid/rules/team-required-tools.md`)
> **Source:** Wickman, *Traction*, Chapters 6 and 7

## How to use this file

This is the **fixed** L10 agenda template. Every weekly L10 follows
this exact structure — same seven items, in the same order, with the
same time allocations. Do not deviate. Do not drop an item. Do not
rearrange. The rhythm is the whole point: the L10 becomes a reflex the
team stops scheduling around, and the format becomes a reflex the team
stops rewriting.

Each week's actual notes live in a separate dated file under
`eos-deliverables/l10-notes/{date}.md` using the companion
`l10-notes-template.md`.

## Rules (from the substance rubric)

- **All 7 agenda items present, in order:** Segue → Scorecard™ Review
  → Rock™ Review → Headlines → To-Do List Review → IDS™ → Conclude.
- **Time allocations:** 5 / 5 / 5 / 5 / 5 / 60 / 5 for a total of 90
  minutes. IDS is the only large block — everything else is tight.
- **Same day of the week, same start time, every week.**
- **90-minute hard stop.** When L10s run over, the thing that always
  gets sacrificed is IDS, which is the segment where real work
  happens.
- **Two assigned roles every week:** a chair who runs the agenda, and
  an agenda manager who keeps the Rock Sheet, Scorecard, To-Do list,
  and Issues List live on the page.
- **Attendance discipline:** the meeting runs even when people are
  missing. Acceptable reasons to miss: vacation or a true emergency.
  Routine skipping is a signal the meeting has stopped being
  load-bearing.

## Agenda — 7 items, fixed times (total 90 min)

### 1. Segue — 5 minutes

Opening ritual. Each person shares one short piece of good news —
business or personal — to shift context from working *in* the business
to working *on* it. Round-robin, short, no discussion.

- **Facilitator note:** move on when the last person has shared. Do
  not turn this into a conversation.

### 2. Scorecard™ Review — 5 minutes

Walk the weekly numbers. Each number is flagged on-track or off-track
against its weekly goal. **Report only — no discussion.** Any
off-track number drops immediately to the Issues List for IDS later
in the meeting.

- **Source artifact:** `scorecard.md`
- **Rule:** suppress the instinct to problem-solve the moment a bad
  number appears. Drop it to IDS and keep moving.

### 3. Rock™ Review — 5 minutes

Walk the Rock Sheet top to bottom: Company Rocks first, then each
leader's individual Rocks. Each Rock owner reports **on-track** or
**off-track** — nothing more. Off-track Rocks drop to IDS. An
on-track Rock can also drop to IDS if someone wants to raise a
concern about it.

- **Source artifact:** `rocks-quarterly.md`
- **Rule:** no discussion in this segment. Status only. Discussion
  belongs in IDS.

### 4. Customer / Employee Headlines — 5 minutes

One-sentence updates on anything significant happening with customers
or employees — good news or bad news. Good news is celebrated in the
moment; bad news becomes an issue and drops to IDS.

- **Facilitator note:** headlines are one sentence. If an update needs
  a paragraph, it is really an issue — add it to the Issues List.

### 5. To-Do List Review — 5 minutes

Walk last week's To-Do list. Each owner reports done or not done. A
To-Do is always a 7-day action item by definition. Target is ~90%
done week over week; a To-Do that has lingered more than two weeks
has probably become an issue and should drop to the Issues List.

- **Rule:** To-Dos are binary. Done is done; anything else is not
  done.

### 6. IDS™ (Identify / Discuss / Solve) — 60 minutes

The core of the meeting. Pick the top three issues from the Issues
List by importance, and work them strictly one at a time in priority
order — **never** top-to-bottom on the page, because the most
important issue is often not the top entry.

For each issue:

- **Identify** — name the real problem. The first stated problem is
  almost never the real one; peel back layers until the root shows
  up. This step often consumes more time than Discuss and Solve
  combined.
- **Discuss** — each person gets one full turn. Repeating a position
  a second time is politicking, not discussion. When points cycle,
  move to Solve. Anyone can call out a drift off-topic; drifting
  threads go to the Issues List or are dropped.
- **Solve** — the output is usually one or more concrete To-Dos with
  named owners, due by next week. Sometimes the output is an
  alignment statement spoken out loud. Sometimes the output is
  "someone owns gathering facts and bringing them back." State the
  solution in one sentence and confirm agreement.

### 7. Conclude — 5 minutes

Three things happen in order:

1. **Read back the new To-Do list.** Every owner confirms their item
   is written down.
2. **Decide what to communicate outside the room** and through which
   channel (company-wide, team-level, one-to-one).
3. **1-to-10 rating.** Each attendee rates the meeting. Standing goal
   is an average above 8. Anything lower triggers a quick diagnostic
   of what to fix next week.

## Non-negotiables for this meeting

- Start on time. Starting late eats IDS.
- End on time. Running over blows up everyone's afternoon and trains
  the team that the L10 is elastic.
- Same day, same time, same agenda, every week.
- Electronic devices off.
- No separate minutes — this agenda document plus the dated notes
  file **is** the record.
- The meeting runs even if people are missing.
