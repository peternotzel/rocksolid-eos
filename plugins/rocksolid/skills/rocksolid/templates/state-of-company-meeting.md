# State-of-the-Company Meeting™

> **Status:** empty_template — filled in before and after each quarterly
> broadcast
> **Team-required:** no — but the audience **is** the full organization,
> not just the leadership team
> **Source:** Wickman, *Traction*, Chapters 3 and 7

## How to use this file

The State-of-the-Company Meeting is the **vision cascade** mechanism:
a short, quarterly broadcast from the leadership team to the entire
workforce. Its job is to repeatedly put the V/TO™ (Vision/Traction
Organizer™) and the current quarter's Company Rocks™ in front of
every employee, so the vision actually reaches the people executing
it. Vision propagates through repetition — this meeting runs every
quarter, not once a year.

## Non-negotiable rules

- **45-minute cap, hard stop.**
- **Full organization invited and expected** — not just leadership.
- **Quarterly cadence**, scheduled at the Quarterly Planning Session™
  that precedes it.
- **Leadership team presents together**, not a single leader reading
  slides.
- **Q&A is open-mic** — any employee, any question, no pre-screening.

## Agenda — 45 minutes total

### 1. Welcome and intent — 3 minutes

Open with the purpose of the meeting in one or two sentences: this is
how leadership keeps the whole company aligned on where we are going
and what we are working on right now. State explicitly that the same
meeting runs every quarter — this is a rhythm, not a one-off.

- **Facilitator note:** do not apologize for the meeting existing.
  Frame it as a commitment leadership owes the team.

### 2. Quick wins from last quarter — 5 minutes

Walk through 3 to 5 specific wins from the quarter that just ended.
Name people and teams where possible. Celebrate in the moment —
applause, specific recognition, visible gratitude.

- **Facilitator note:** not a humble-brag about revenue. Real wins
  that show the team doing the work: a difficult client saved, a
  process improved, a new hire nailed onboarding, a Rock completed
  against odds.

### 3. Walk through the V/TO Vision page — 10 minutes

Re-state the slow-changing pieces of the vision so every employee
hears them again:

- **Core Values** — name each one and briefly describe the behavior
  it points at
- **Core Focus™** — the Purpose/Cause/Passion, and the Niche
- **10-Year Target™** — the single long-range goal with its dated
  horizon and concrete number

Do not try to cover the full Marketing Strategy or 3-Year Picture™ in
detail — reference them and point employees to where they can read
the full V/TO if they want to.

- **Facilitator note:** employees need to hear these multiple times
  before they become part of their mental model. Repetition is the
  feature.

### 4. This quarter's Company Rocks — 10 minutes

Walk through each of this quarter's Company Rocks. For each one:

- What the Rock is, in one sentence
- Who owns it
- Why it matters
- What "done" looks like at quarter-end

Individual Rocks from the leadership team are **not** shared here —
only the Company Rocks. The principle of "less is more" applies: the
room should leave with 3 to 7 priorities in mind, not a list of 40.

### 5. Why these Rocks matter for the V/TO — 5 minutes

Connect the dots upward. For each Company Rock, name which part of
the V/TO it is moving forward — which 1-Year Plan goal, which 3-Year
Picture bullet, which step toward the 10-Year Target. The cascade
should be visible, not implicit.

- **Facilitator note:** employees disengage when Rocks feel
  disconnected from the bigger picture. Making the linkage explicit is
  what converts the Rocks into shared purpose.

### 6. Q&A — 10 minutes

Open mic. Any employee can ask any question. Leadership team answers
honestly — "I don't know but I'll find out by {date}" is a better
answer than a manufactured one.

- **Facilitator note:** if a question opens into a longer issue, park
  it on the short-term Issues List and commit to following up rather
  than derailing the 45-minute cap.

### 7. Closing — 2 minutes

Three things:

1. Thank the team for showing up
2. Confirm the next State-of-the-Company Meeting date (approximately
   90 days out)
3. Point employees to the follow-up Q&A channel for anything they
   didn't get to ask live

## After the meeting

- [ ] Meeting notes captured (at minimum: wins shared, Rocks shared,
      questions asked and answers given)
- [ ] Recording posted if applicable
- [ ] Follow-up answers to any "I'll find out" questions sent within
      the week
- [ ] Next State-of-the-Company Meeting date added to the company
      calendar
- [ ] Any issues raised during Q&A moved onto the short-term or
      long-term Issues List

## Why this meeting exists

Paraphrased: people need to hear a vision many times before it
actually becomes part of how they think about the business. A
single unveiling at the end of a V/TO session reaches the leadership
team and nobody else. The State-of-the-Company Meeting is the forum
where vision broadcast becomes routine — the only way vision
actually propagates across headcount.
