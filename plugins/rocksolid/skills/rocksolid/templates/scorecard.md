# Scorecard™

> **Status:** empty_template — filled in during the leadership team's
> Scorecard-setting session, then updated weekly
> **Team-required:** yes (see `rocksolid/rules/team-required-tools.md`)
> **Source:** Wickman, *Traction*, Chapter 5 — The Data Component

## How to use this file

The Scorecard is a single rolling grid of 5 to 15 weekly numbers that
lets the leadership team predict what is about to happen before it
shows up in the financials. The numbers are **activity-based leading
indicators** — not trailing P&L figures. A P&L tells you what already
happened; by the time you read it, the past is unchangeable. The
Scorecard exists to move the team *before* outcomes move, while there
is still time to act.

Each row has exactly one named owner from the Accountability Chart™.
The "WHO" column is deliberately the leftmost column on the grid so
that ownership is unavoidable at a glance — a number nobody owns is a
number nobody actually drives.

## Rules (from the substance rubric)

- **5 to 15 weekly numbers**, with closer to 5 preferred. Below 5 means
  you're missing leading indicators. Above 15 turns the weekly review
  segment into noise.
- **Activity-based, not P&L-based.** Each number must move *before*
  the financial outcome it predicts moves. Revenue last week is a
  trailing number; sales-calls-made last week is a leading number.
- **Exactly one named owner per number** — the person heading the
  function that drives the number, not the finance person who fills
  in the spreadsheet.
- **Each number has an explicit weekly goal**, not only an actual.
  Without a goal there is nothing to red-flag against.
- **Rolling 13-week format** with dated week-ending columns. Not
  monthly. Not quarterly summary. 13 weeks is the right window to
  spot a trend — one off-week is noise; three off-weeks in a row is
  a signal.
- **Red-flag any weekly miss.** A visible marker on the missed cell
  pulls the eye during the L10™ Scorecard Review segment and drops
  the number to the Issues List for IDS™.
- **WHO column is leftmost**, structurally elevated.
- **Goals tie to the 1-Year Plan** (V/TO™, Phase 2+). In Phase 1 — no
  V/TO yet — goals come from a recorded pragmatic team target and are
  realigned once the 1-Year Plan exists.

## The "desert island" framing (how to pick the numbers)

Before writing a single row, the leadership team runs a thought
experiment: imagine you were suddenly cut off from all communication
with the business — no email, no phone, no visits — and had to run
the company from a single sheet of paper containing a handful of
weekly numbers. Which numbers would *have* to be on that sheet?

The exercise forces the team to strip everything that is
nice-to-know and surface the tiny set of numbers that genuinely
matter. This is why the final Scorecard is closer to 5 rows than to
15 — the weekly review is only valuable if every number on the page
earns its place.

## Notes

### How the Scorecard evolves

The first draft will be about 85% right. Do not try to design the
perfect Scorecard in one sitting — the right numbers surface over
one to three months of weekly use. Expect to prune some rows, replace
others, and sharpen the goals on a few more. The version of the
Scorecard the team ends up relying on is almost never the one they
wrote in the first session.

### How it ties to the V/TO™ 1-Year Plan

Once the V/TO exists (Phase 2), each weekly goal on this grid should
trace back to a 1-Year Plan target. A weekly number whose goal has
no linkage upward tends to drift — it was picked for a reason the
team has since forgotten.

In Phase 1 (pre-V/TO), goals are pragmatic targets the leadership
team agreed on. Record the rationale so the Phase 2 realignment can
check what was intentional vs. what was a placeholder.
