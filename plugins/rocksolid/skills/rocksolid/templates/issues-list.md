# Issues List™

> **Status:** empty_template — populated continuously between meetings
> **Team-required:** no (anyone on the leadership team can add an item
> at any time; the L10™ is where items get worked)
> **Source:** Wickman, *Traction*, Chapter 6 — The Issues Component

## How to use this file

The Issues List is **not** a meeting artifact you generate at the
start of the L10. It is a continuous queue that lives between
meetings. Anyone on the leadership team can — and should — drop an
item onto it the moment it surfaces during the week: a bad Scorecard™
number, an off-track Rock™, a customer signal, a frustrated team
member, a tangent that came up during another discussion. At each
weekly L10 the team picks the **top three** by importance and works
them strictly in priority order through the IDS™ track (Identify /
Discuss / Solve).

This file holds two distinct queues:

- **Short-term list** — strategic issues that belong at the leadership
  level and are worked in the weekly L10.
- **Long-term list** — horizon issues for the V/TO™ that are reviewed
  at quarterly and annual planning sessions, not this week.

Keep them separate. Mixing them is the single most common mistake.

## Rules (from the substance rubric)

- **Continuous, not meeting-generated.** Items appear between
  meetings, not only at the meeting itself. If every entry has the
  same timestamp as the L10, the discipline is not in place.
- **Three types must be declared up front.** Before an item is worked
  in IDS, the person raising it states which of the three types it is
  (see below). Resolution paths differ.
- **Top three by priority each week, never top-to-bottom.** The
  most important issue is often not the top entry on the page.
  Working down the list by order of entry wastes the IDS hour.
- **Short-term and long-term lists are separate.** Long-term items
  belong to the V/TO Issues List and do not get worked in the weekly
  L10.
- **Tangents during IDS land here.** When the team drifts off-topic,
  the drift becomes a new line item here and the team returns to the
  current issue.
- **Unsolved items persist.** An issue that isn't reached this week
  stays on the list for next week — it does not evaporate.

## The three types of issue

Naming the type before the team starts Identify prevents the group
from chasing a solution when what's actually needed is alignment or
a green light.

1. **Problem to solve** — something is broken or off-track and action
   is needed. Resolution is usually a To-Do with a named owner.
2. **Information to communicate** — a piece of context the team needs
   to acknowledge. Resolution is alignment, stated out loud, with no
   action required.
3. **Idea or opportunity needing feedback** — a proposal that needs
   either a green light, a red light, or a request for more research.
   Resolution is a decision, not a task.

## Notes

- **Drift handling:** any item that surfaces mid-IDS as a tangent
  either lands on this list (if it is a real issue on its own) or
  is dropped.
- **To-Do list boundary:** an IDS solution is usually one or more
  To-Dos. Those live on next week's L10 To-Do list, not here.
- **Departmental vs leadership scope:** operational issues that belong
  to a single department should be pushed down to that department's
  own weekly meeting — do not solve them in the leadership L10.
