## How to use this file

This is the generic template for one core process. Every core
process in the organization uses the same format — that's what makes
the compiled package (the Core Processes binder / wiki) legible end
to end.

The target length is **2 to 10 pages**. Under 2 pages and you
haven't transferred enough working knowledge; over 10 and you've
slipped into over-documentation that nobody will read. Operations
processes tend to land on the longer end of the range; administrative
processes land on the shorter end.

## The 20/80 rule (read before drafting)

Capture the **20%** of the content that drives **80%** of the
results. The format is deliberately high-level: **major numbered
steps with a handful of bullet sub-steps under each step**. Not
flowcharts. Not swimlane diagrams. Not keystroke-level instructions.
Not procedural code. Not "leave no judgment to the executor."

The purpose is to make sure the people executing the process are not
**skipping structural steps**, because skipped steps are the root
cause of most downstream problems. Checklists and detailed work
instructions can live as attachments if needed — they are not this
document.

## Rules (from the substance rubric)

- **Owner is a seat on the Accountability Chart™.** A process with
  no owning seat has no accountable party when it drifts.
- **2 to 10 pages in length.** Under 2, add structural detail;
  over 10, cut to 20/80.
- **Major numbered steps with bullet sub-steps** — the format is
  fixed.
- **Sanity-read by someone other than the owner.** A single-author
  process risks encoding how one person thinks about it, not how the
  work actually happens.
- **Named consistently** with the leadership team's agreed list. No
  departmental variants. No legacy aliases.
