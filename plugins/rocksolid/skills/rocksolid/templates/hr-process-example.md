## Purpose — one sentence

To move every person who joins this company from the moment a seat
opens, through hire, through their first 90 days, and into an
ongoing review rhythm that either keeps them thriving in the right
seat or surfaces the wrong-seat problem early enough to fix it.

## Scope

- **Starts when:** a seat on the Accountability Chart becomes open
  (new seat created, incumbent moving, or incumbent exiting).
- **Ends when:** the new seat holder is running in steady state with
  an ongoing quarterly review cadence.
- **Does NOT include:** compensation structure decisions,
  benefits-program design, payroll mechanics, or termination-for-cause
  events. Those sit in separate core processes (Compensation,
  Benefits Administration, and Conduct & Separation respectively).
- **Related core processes:** Accountability Chart Review,
  Compensation, Conduct & Separation, Team Operating Rhythms.

## Major steps

### Step 1 — Define the open seat

- Confirm the seat exists on the current Accountability Chart and
  that its 5 major roles are still accurate. If they are stale,
  revise them before posting anything.
- Write a one-page seat brief: the 5 major roles, the scope, the
  primary measurable(s) the seat owns, and the leader this seat
  reports to.
- Agree the People Analyzer™ bar for Core Values this seat must
  meet, and the GWC™ expectation (Get it / Want it / Capacity).
- Decide whether this is a leadership-team seat or a
  non-leadership seat — the answer drives compensation band,
  interview loop, and onboarding depth.

### Step 2 — Source candidates

- Publish the seat brief to the sourcing channels that historically
  return the highest quality-per-applicant for this kind of role.
- Ask current team members first: internal referrals from people
  who already meet the Core Values bar are the highest-yield
  channel and should be exhausted before paid channels.
- Keep a running list of passive candidates (people worth talking
  to when a seat opens). Refresh the list quarterly, even when no
  seats are open.
- Track source-channel yield — how many applicants came from each
  channel, and how many converted to hire. Underperforming
  channels get retired.

### Step 3 — Screen and shortlist

- A first-pass screen removes candidates who do not meet the basic
  seat brief (location, legal-to-work, minimum capability floor).
  This is binary and fast.
- A 20-to-30-minute phone screen covers motivation, a narrative of
  the candidate's last two roles, and three calibration questions
  against the seat's measurable.
- Shortlist the top 3 to 5 candidates for the structured interview
  loop. Record a one-paragraph rationale per shortlisted candidate
  so the loop can calibrate on what to probe.
- Anything less than 3 candidates on the shortlist is itself a
  signal — either the seat brief is off, the sourcing channels are
  wrong, or the compensation is out of market.

### Step 4 — Structured interview loop (three rounds)

- **Round 1 — Seat fit.** The hiring manager and one peer run a
  45-minute interview against the 5 major roles of the seat. The
  goal is to test whether the candidate can actually own those
  roles, using concrete past-behavior questions.
- **Round 2 — Core Values and GWC calibration.** Two leadership
  team members run a 45-minute interview explicitly against the
  Core Values and the three GWC axes. Each interviewer scores
  independently and the pair debriefs before the next round.
- **Round 3 — Work sample or domain deep-dive.** A real,
  representative task the seat holder will actually do — reviewed
  live or async. This is the round that catches the
  interviews-well-but-doesn't-execute pattern.
- **Debrief within 24 hours.** All interviewers sit together, each
  gives a recommendation (hire / no-hire / need more data), and the
  hiring manager makes the decision. Split decisions default to
  no-hire.

### Step 5 — References, offer, and acceptance

- Do at least two live reference calls — never only email
  references, and never only references the candidate hand-picked.
  The most useful references are often someone the candidate worked
  next to, not their former manager.
- Each reference call follows the same four-question script:
  concrete example of the candidate at their best, concrete
  example of them at their worst, fit for the specific seat we are
  hiring into, and would you work with them again.
- Make the offer verbally first, then send the written offer
  within 24 hours. Verbal first lets you negotiate in real time
  without a paper trail that locks the conversation.
- If the candidate declines, capture why in one sentence — that
  feedback improves the seat brief, the sourcing, or the
  compensation band next time.

### Step 6 — Onboarding and the first 90 days

- **Day 1 — welcome and orientation.** Workstation ready, team
  introductions done, V/TO™ and Accountability Chart walked through
  so the new hire can see where their seat fits in the whole.
- **Week 1 — context loading.** Meetings with each functional head,
  read-throughs of the relevant core processes, and a written
  "first impressions" note from the new hire at end of week.
- **Day 30 check-in.** Hiring manager and new hire sit down for a
  one-hour conversation — is the seat what was described, what is
  confusing, what does the new hire need to be successful? Any
  surprises on either side get named now.
- **Day 60 check-in.** Review progress against the seat's
  measurable(s), and do a mid-point GWC pulse check. This is the
  last chance to catch a wrong-seat situation cheaply.
- **Day 90 confirmation.** Full People Analyzer review against
  Core Values and GWC. Either the new hire graduates to ongoing
  rhythm (Step 7) or the wrong-seat conversation happens here
  while everyone still has fresh eyes on it.

### Step 7 — Ongoing quarterly reviews

- Every seat holder has a 30-to-45-minute quarterly review with
  their leader on a fixed cadence tied to the quarterly planning
  rhythm. Reviews are not annual — annual is too slow.
- Each review walks the same four sections: Core Values (People
  Analyzer refresh), GWC on the current seat, progress against the
  seat's weekly number, and one personal-growth item the person is
  working on.
- Reviews are **conversations**, not form-fills — the artifact is
  a short written summary that both parties sign. The summary
  lives in the person's file.
- Any two-consecutive-quarter dip below the People Analyzer bar or
  the GWC bar triggers the Conduct & Separation core process
  (which is a different document), not a performance-improvement
  plan held inside this process.
